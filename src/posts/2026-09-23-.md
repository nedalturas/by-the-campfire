> "The only true wisdom is in knowing you know nothing."\
> — *Socrates*

We only perceive what we allow ourselves to grasp. Every action is built upon preconceived thought notions and socially accepted truths embedded deep within humanity.

Though it makes me wonder, are we merely fools under the guise of an intellectual?

We walk through this world convinced we are looking through a clear glass window, when in truth we are looking into a mirror dirtied with the dust of everything that we think we know.

Rarely does it shatter, though, as people would rather look at their distorted and deceptive reflection than to let their belief fracture into fragments of truth they don't want to swallow.

We pride ourselves as the apex predator, standing on top amongst creatures with fangs and claws, due to our capacity to think. Animals act on instincts and habits to survive, they do not construct elaborate systems of denial to protect their fragile ego.

The fortress walls that we call society dampen the curious intelligence of mankind, blanketing the mind with comfortable lies. We have reached planets and stars, observed the cosmos, yet remain largely shackled with societal standards.

We thus have fallen into a deeper trap than ferity ever could: we have the hubris of assuming intelligence is wisdom, and that assumption is what let curiosity fade.

Yet curiosity, once smothered, does not simply vanish, it leaves a vacancy that becomes complacency. It does not arrive as an enemy, it arrives as comfort, as convenience, as the relief of no longer having to ask.

Society, and those who profit from our stillness, do not need to conquer a mind that has already stopped defending itself.

The truth, twisted by cruel thinkers acting as leaders and enlighteners to a greater tomorrow, fattens us like sheep, not for our nourishment, but for the harvest of their gains.

Institutions have no need for iron chains for minds already convinced to stay. Mandating the pasture is enough, curating what we see, follow, and what is true today only to be discarded tomorrow. Every boundary arrives draped in the language of the collective good, quietly concealing its purpose.

This is the genius of the modern leash, as it does not demand obedience, it manufactures consent. A ruler who must force compliance has already failed, but a ruler who is thanked for the chains has succeeded completely.

Recency dressed as relevance, popularity dressed as truth, and so the herd moves as one, guided not by coercion, but by habit.

But a mirror, no matter how dirtied, can still be wiped clean by the hands willing to reach for it. The wooly blanket of comfort is warm and heavy, as it is meant to keep us stagnant.

Comfort is not the enemy, complacency is.

To be complacent enough not to seek truth for yourself is to leave it buried beneath the throne of lies where you sit comfortably. To seek the truth may be a tough endeavor, but it does not reject what we're taught or what we know so far. All that it asks is for us not to accept it unexamined, let belief step out of the darkness of falsehoods, and hold it up to the light of truth.

Not to be certain, but to be willing to hold what you know loosely enough to still question it.

Socrates did not counsel us to know nothing, only that we do not yet know, and never will know everything. But to keep searching for it in your own right, thinking outside the herd, the trends, and the cave we built ourselves with complacency.

You only need to ask, quietly and often: is this true because I have found it to be so, or true because I was simply told?

Perhaps that is enough. Not to reject the knowledge that made us who we are, but to stand firm within it with clear eyes, and choose the truth for ourselves.
