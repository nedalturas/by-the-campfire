Forgive me, for I have sinned.



I once attempted to write down every sin I had ever committed,

but found the task rather beyond me.

It seems a man may exhaust his ink

long before he exhausts his guilt.



Perhaps it would take several nights

to count the stars and call each one by name;

perhaps forty-two folds of paper

would suffice to contain the weight of my soul.

I cannot say which is the greater absurdity

the number of my sins,

or my desperate belief that naming them

might somehow make me innocent.



For I was told that confession mends the heart,

that what is brought into the light

loses some of its power to haunt us.

And so I began to write,

with all the solemnity of a condemned man

preparing his final defence.



Yet even as the words gathered upon the page,

a rather inconvenient question occurred to me:



To whom, precisely, does one confess

when one has forgotten how to be honest,

even with oneself?



I have the sins.

I have the paper.

I have the ink.



But I know not to whom this confession ought to be sent.
