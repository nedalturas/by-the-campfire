I bore a crystalline crown of regality,
certain the whole world was mine to claim.
Sitting on the golden throne so loftily,
my scornful lenses hid my own quiet shame.

We waged our wars loudly across the board,
A bitter feud thinking we were unjustly wronged.
Tearing us apart with words sharper than a sword,
A losing battle where neither of us belonged.

I thought myself the pillar, unyieldingly strong,
while you were nothing but a story written wrong.
The family called you lazy, disappointing and slow,
I took your silence as proof that it was so.

In time, the crystalline crown shattered unbid,
the lies that blinded me were suddenly revealed.
Bringing to light the shadows the luster hid,
a woman strong enough who never had to yield.


I was the failure, fragile down to the bone,
blinded by the lies in which my flaws had grown.
Your silence was never weakness, but quiet grace,
a truth they buried, but time could not erase.
