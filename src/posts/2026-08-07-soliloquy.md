Tell me, is there still room in you for forgiveness? If I were to crawl closer, dragging my wretchedness to your feet only to lash out with some vulgarity, would you understand it?

I have lived inside a falsehood for so long that it has become my shell; I try to speak a truth now, but I find my tongue no longer knows the shape of it.

I wake too early, just in time to hear the world begin without me. I put on the clothes I wore yesterday and wish for a life that does not require bargaining with time.

Then I repeat what I am expected to repeat. I work because everyone else works. I smile with enough precision that no one asks whether I am well. It is a useful smile. It saves explanations.

No one notices that, beneath it, something has been dying for a very long time.

Formerly, I occupied my hours building elaborate fortresses against the unexpected, only to find the enemy was already inside. Now I sit at the table all day, chewing on the very seeds of doubt that have taken root in my head.

I keep telling myself that the better days belong to the future, waiting with a patience I cannot imitate. Yet my mind refuses their invitation. It returns, with mechanical devotion, to the past—not to remember it, but to rehearse the mistakes as though repetition might alter what has already been decided.

Lying has become an ordinary part of living. I no longer know whether I do it to deceive other people or to remain recognizable to them. The truth is too small to survive in conversation.

If I ever said it aloud, it would only be one sentence:

**I am a mess.**

Strange how an entire life can be reduced to four words, and still they remain the most difficult words to speak.

I have labored so diligently to construct myself entirely out of the needs of those around me. I made my physical body a prop for their weeping, and I gladly played the clown, hoping the laughter might anchor me to this world. I tried so desperately to forge myself into something useful, something good.

But the mold shattered. The heavy, protective armor of the 'friend' I tried to cast around myself has fallen away, revealing only the hollow space where I was supposed to be.

But a strange miracle occurred while I was suffocating beneath my clownish disguise: I found myself in the company of truly unmasked souls. Their untruths are not the heavy, suffocating concealments I am accustomed to, but rather soft, understandable defenses that only make them dearer to me.

They care for others with an ease that absolutely baffles my rigid mind. And there are some who cast a gaze upon me that suddenly anchors me to the earth—making me feel, against all my own convictions, that I am desired. We wander through different cities, tasting meals at unknown tables, distracting ourselves with joyous things so that, for a precious moment, the crushing weight of the world might forget to look for us.

How am I supposed to sleep when doubt never grows tired? Fear waits beside the bed, and dread is always the first thing to wake.

*"How am I?"*

It is a simple question with no honest answer.

I have a beautiful life, yet I remain unsatisfied. I thought adulthood would feel like freedom, but instead it has become an exhausting negotiation with myself.

If I am being honest, I cannot tell people that this is all I ever wanted. Most nights, I struggle to sleep—not because I am restless, but because the house is haunted by thoughts that refuse to let me go.

What right do I have to enter the asylum of sleep when my internal court has declared an eternal session? The relentless magistrates of my own mind continue to read out charges of doubt and fear, refusing to grant an adjournment simply because the sun has set.

I keep calling it self-awareness, as if giving it a better name might redeem it. But it is only self-obsession, and I have become its most faithful prisoner.

I have watched the vitality of my youth fade into absolute nothingness, trapped inside the fever of an unwell mind. And today, I am delivered the most devastating truth of all: that relief was always within reach, that a remedy was possible.

To discover the cure only after the spring of one's life has turned to dust... I can conceive of no greater agony. It is a true purgatory to be shown the path to health only when one no longer has the youth to walk down it.

If you want to know how I am, you have already read the answer.
